What the Laser Does

Our laser uses a beam to cut or engrave materials like wood, acrylic, cardboard, and leather. It's best with smooth vector shapes, and it can also etch photos (though quality may vary).

How to Create Laser-Ready Artwork

  1. Choose your material & size your canvas. The Dream Center does not provide material. Measure width × height and note thickness and material type. Suitable materials: acrylic, wood sheets, leather, or painted stainless steel (Stanley/Yeti-style cups).
  2. Plan your operations. Each line needs one of three roles:
    • Engrave = fill areas (logos, photos, solid shapes)
    • Score = thin vector lines for light marks/detail
    • Cut = vector "hairline" paths that go all the way through
    Tip: color-code by role (e.g., Black = engrave, Blue = score, Red = cut) so power/speed is easy to set later.
  3. Pick your design tool. Beginner: Inkscape (free) or Canva. Intermediate: Adobe Illustrator or Affinity Designer. For photos: Photos (Mac), GIMP, or Photoshop.
  4. Build the artwork with a Dream Center partner using xTool's Creative Space software.
    • Vectors: clean paths without overlaps; join/merge where necessary
    • Engravings: filled shapes or high-contrast bitmap images (black/white)
    • Photos: crop tightly, convert to grayscale, boost contrast/clarity, apply dithering (e.g. Floyd-Steinberg), target 300–600 DPI at final size
    • Stencils: add bridges on letters like A, O, and R so centers don't fall out
  5. Preflight checklist before sending to the laser:
    • Size matches your material
    • Text is outlined; no hidden layers
    • No duplicate or stacked cut lines
    • Cut strokes = hairline; score strokes = thin but not hairline; engravings = fills
    • Raster images are pure black-and-white at 300–600 DPI
    • QR codes tested (print on paper first to size and scan)

Tips & Tricks

  • Simple, high-contrast art wins — busy gradients don't engrave well
  • Paper prototype first: print to scale and lay it on your material
  • For press-fit parts, leave slight clearance; kerf-test for a perfect friction fit
  • Score lines: use single-line (centerline) or "stick" fonts for clean, fast detail
  • Wood engraving: masking tape can reduce smoke staining — peel after
  • Cannot engrave unsafe materials (e.g. PVC/vinyl) — ask staff if unsure
